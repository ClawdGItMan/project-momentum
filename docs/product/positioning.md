# Positioning

> **Updated March 2026** — informed by user research survey (n=26). See [survey results](../research/survey-results-march-2026.md).

## One-Line Positioning

Outtcast is a social self-improvement app where real progress is shared with the people who actually care — not the public.

## Category

Intimate social accountability for personal growth.

## Problem

People track improvement in isolated apps, then share life online in environments optimized for distraction, status signaling, and passive consumption. The result is fragmented progress and weak accountability.

## Alternative Behaviors

- use Strava or Whoop for narrow metrics (survey: "community tabs feel dry")
- use a habit tracker privately (survey: "it's just you holding yourself accountable")
- post progress on Instagram, TikTok, or Snapchat (survey: feels performative / cringe)
- keep goals to yourself and rely on willpower (survey: 62% don't share anywhere today)

## Why Outtcast Wins

- it combines social identity with meaningful progress
- it centers consistency and accountability over vanity — in small trusted groups (survey: 85%)
- it can pull in data from existing tools (Apple Health first, 62% demand)
- nobody else occupies the intersection of accountability + intimacy + multi-dimensional growth
- users naturally describe it as "lifestyle" not "fitness" — broader than competitors

## Messaging Pillars

### Make progress visible

Show the work, not just the outcome.

### Make growth social

Friends and peers should help users stay consistent.

### Make effort matter

Reward people for doing the work, not for gaming an algorithm.

## Positioning Guardrails

- avoid sounding like a generic productivity app
- avoid hyper-masculine or exclusionary self-improvement language
- avoid claiming to fix every area of life on day one
- avoid overemphasizing rankings before the product earns trust
