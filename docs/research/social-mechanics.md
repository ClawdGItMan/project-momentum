# Social Mechanics Research

## Core Hypothesis

The product becomes sticky when users feel seen by people whose opinion matters to them. Social mechanics should reinforce identity, accountability, and encouragement rather than maximize passive scrolling.

## Mechanics To Explore

### Follow graph

- friend follows for accountability
- aspirational follows for creators and role models
- mixed feed potential, but avoid making the first experience too noisy

### Daily progress feed

- short, structured updates beat generic status posts
- progress posts should combine data, habits, and reflections
- reactions should signal support more than popularity chasing

### Profile as momentum board

- profile should communicate what someone is working on
- highlight consistency, current streaks, focus pillars, and recent wins
- make the profile feel alive even without perfect content volume

### Visibility rules

- stronger visibility should come from actual effort and consistency
- avoid opaque algorithmic favoritism in the earliest versions
- creator fairness matters, but the app must first work for regular users

### Social guardrails

- avoid infinite-scroll addiction patterns where possible
- keep post creation lightweight so action beats lurking
- encourage reflection and support, not performative comparison

## Open Tensions

- close-friends accountability versus open discovery
- structured posts versus creative expression
- visibility based on consistency versus visibility based on social engagement

## Signal

Users are more likely to return if the social layer feels purpose-built for progress, not like a copy of mainstream feed behavior pasted onto self-improvement content.

## Implication

Project Momentum should use a more structured, effort-centered social model where posting progress and supporting others is the default interaction loop.

## Next Step

Interview users about what they would actually want to see in a daily progress feed and what kinds of social reactions feel motivating rather than corny.
