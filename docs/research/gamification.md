# Gamification Research

## Design Goal

Gamification should make users feel momentum, not manipulation. The system has to reward consistency and honest effort without pushing people into unhealthy comparison loops.

## Principles Worth Testing

### Reward baseline-relative progress

Users should feel they can win against their own previous self. This lowers discouragement and broadens the addressable audience beyond elite performers.

### Celebrate consistency over spikes

Daily or weekly consistency should matter more than rare standout performances. The app should make showing up feel valuable.

### Keep early wins easy to understand

The first few sessions need to feel rewarding fast. New users should quickly see progress badges, completed habits, or visible profile momentum.

### Reserve heavy ranking for later

Public ladders are emotionally powerful but easy to get wrong. For v0.1, visible progress summaries and milestone badges are safer than a highly tuned global ranking system.

### Avoid empty dopamine mechanics

If a reward cannot be tied to real action or real support, it probably should not exist.

## Research Questions

- what milestone system feels motivating without looking childish?
- how should likes, comments, and support affect visible progress, if at all?
- what is the right balance between private goals and public proof?
- how transparent should the scoring model be?

## Working Recommendation

For the prototype, use simple signals:

- streaks or consistency counts
- milestone badges
- visible profile momentum
- optional encouragement reactions

Keep XP and advanced leveling conceptual for now unless a simple version materially improves the demo.

## Signal

The most promising mechanic is not a flashy ranking ladder; it is a clear sense that consistent action creates visible status and momentum over time.

## Implication

The first prototype should prioritize progress visibility and milestone feedback, while postponing complex ranking math until user motivation patterns are better understood.

## Next Step

Collect examples from habit apps, fitness apps, and games, then map which mechanics encourage healthy repetition versus shallow compulsion.
